About Me

I'm a Computer Engineering student at Georgia Tech with a minor in Mathematics, graduating December 2027. My work spans a wide range: I've written firmware in C++ for microcontrollers, designed digital circuits in SystemVerilog for a real silicon tapeout, built full-stack web apps, and trained deep learning models on GPU clusters. That breadth isn't accidental — I genuinely believe the most interesting engineering happens at the seams between disciplines.

My coursework reflects that curiosity. Beyond the standard CS theory (Data Structures, Algorithms, Discrete Math), I've taken Intro to Embedded Systems, GPU Programming, Malware Reverse Engineering, Computer Security, and Advanced Malware Analysis. Each of those courses pushed me to think about computing at a different layer of abstraction — from silicon timing constraints all the way up to application-layer protocol analysis.

Outside the classroom, I've had the chance to work on research and engineering problems that actually matter. At the MARGA Lab, I built infrastructure around LEO satellite constellations to explore positioning in GPS-denied environments — work that required me to get comfortable with orbital mechanics, AWS infrastructure, and CI/CD pipelines all at once. At Jane Street, I sharpened my algorithmic thinking by building trading bots, entropy-based solvers, and Monte Carlo simulations under real competitive pressure. And at the University of Washington, I got an early look at the efficiency frontier of neuromorphic computing — a space I expect to matter a lot as energy constraints on AI hardware grow tighter.

When I'm not at a keyboard, I'm usually thinking about the next project. Right now that means exploring the overlap between hardware security and machine learning — two fields that are increasingly colliding in ways the industry isn't fully prepared for. If that sounds interesting to you too, I'd love to talk.
